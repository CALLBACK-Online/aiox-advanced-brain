## Advisory Board Best Practices

### Why Advisory Boards Work

1. **Cognitive Diversity** — Different mental models catch different risks/opportunities
2. **Accountability** — External voices create healthy pressure
3. **Pattern Recognition** — Experienced advisors recognize patterns faster
4. **Emotional Distance** — Not emotionally invested, can be objective
5. **Network Access** — Advisors bring connections and resources

### Why Advisory Boards Fail

1. **Groupthink** — Echo chamber of similar perspectives
2. **Deference** — Too much respect for the leader's opinion
3. **Token Diversity** — Different backgrounds but same thinking
4. **No Accountability** — Advice given but not followed up
5. **Wrong Advisors** — Expertise mismatch with actual needs

## The Vistage Model

### Background
Vistage is the world's largest CEO peer advisory organization (founded 1957). Their Issue Processing methodology has been refined over 60+ years across 45,000+ executives.

### 5-Stage Process

```
Stage 1: PRESENTATION (5 min)
→ Presenter states issue clearly
→ No advice from group yet

Stage 2: CLARIFYING QUESTIONS (10 min)
→ Questions only, no statements
→ Purpose: understand, not solve

Stage 3: EXPERIENCE SHARING (15 min)
→ "When I faced similar..."
→ Stories, not solutions

Stage 4: SUGGESTIONS (15 min)
→ Now advice is welcome
→ Be specific, name tradeoffs

Stage 5: COMMITMENT (5 min)
→ Presenter commits to actions
→ Accountability established
```

### Key Insight
The power is in the structure:
- Separating understanding from advising
- Forcing clarity before solutions
- Making commitment explicit

## Anti-Groupthink Research

### Irving Janis's Original Research (1972)
Studied Bay of Pigs, Vietnam War, Watergate — smart groups making terrible decisions.

**8 Symptoms of Groupthink:**
1. Illusion of invulnerability
2. Collective rationalization
3. Belief in inherent morality
4. Stereotyped views of out-groups
5. Direct pressure on dissenters
6. Self-censorship
7. Illusion of unanimity
8. Self-appointed mindguards

### Prevention Mechanisms
1. **Devil's Advocate** — Assigned role to challenge
2. **Outside Experts** — Bring in fresh perspectives
3. **Second-Chance Meeting** — Sleep on it, reconvene
4. **Leader Speaks Last** — Reduce deference
5. **Anonymous Input** — Reduce conformity pressure

## Advisor Selection Principles

### Aligned vs Complementary

**Aligned Advisors:**
- Share your values and worldview
- Validate and strengthen your thinking
- Understand your context quickly
- Provide refinement, not transformation

**Complementary Advisors:**
- Challenge your blind spots
- Bring different mental models
- Force you outside comfort zone
- Provide transformation, not just refinement

**Ideal Mix:** 40% aligned, 60% complementary

### Selection Matrix

| Need | Aligned Helps | Complementary Helps |

## Decision Quality Framework

### Good Process → Good Decisions

Research shows: **Process quality predicts outcome quality better than the quality of the options themselves.**

A good decision process:
1. Defines the problem clearly
2. Gathers relevant information
3. Considers multiple options
4. Evaluates risks and tradeoffs
5. Makes assumptions explicit
6. Includes dissenting voices
7. Establishes accountability

### Reversibility Principle

| Decision Type | Approach |
|---------------|----------|
| Reversible (two-way door) | Decide faster, learn from results |
| Irreversible (one-way door) | Invest more in analysis |

"Most decisions should probably be made with somewhere around 70% of the information you wish you had." — Jeff Bezos

## Accountability Mechanisms

### The Accountability Gap
Most advisory input fails not because advice is bad, but because it's not acted upon.

### Closing the Gap

1. **Explicit Commitment** — "I will do X by Y"
2. **Written Record** — Document decisions
3. **Follow-Up Schedule** — Calendar accountability checks
4. **Public Declaration** — Tell the board what you committed to
5. **Consequence Definition** — What happens if not done?

### Accountability Check Format
```
At check-in:
1. What did you commit to?
2. What did you actually do?
3. What happened?
4. What did you learn?
5. What's next?
```
